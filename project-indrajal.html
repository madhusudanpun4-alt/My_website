Technical project · 2025

Indrajal STOL Firefighting Aircraft Design

XFLR5 X-Plane Stability Analysis Conceptual Design

Overview

Indrajal is a twin-engine STOL (Short Take-Off and Landing) utility aircraft, designed to assist in controlling and suppressing wildfires by delivering water or retardant directly onto burning areas. It carries a two-person crew — pilot and co-pilot — and uses a pressurized, belly-mounted drop system with compressed air to push water or retardant through the nozzles, allowing operation across diverse altitudes and challenging terrain.

Mission & Design Requirements

The mission profile covers take-off, climb, cruise to the fire zone, a retardant drop, and return to base — with the aircraft required to operate from short or rough airstrips close to active fire zones. Key design requirements included:

  • STOL performance with a cruise ceiling of 4,800 m
  • Cruise speed of 75 m/s
  • Two-crew configuration (pilot and co-pilot)
  • Reliable handling across varied altitude and loading conditions

Aircraft Specification

The final configuration is a high-wing, twin-engine design (2 × 650 hp) with an 8,400 kg MTOW, 3,600 kg empty weight, and a 3,500 kg payload capacity for water or retardant. It has a 500 km range and 2-hour endurance, a 20 m wingspan (NACA 2412 airfoil, aspect ratio 7.81), and NACA 0008 horizontal and vertical stabilizers. Take-off distance came out to 426 m and landing distance to 518 m — comfortably within STOL territory for operating near fire zones.

Loading & Drop System

  • Water fill rate of approximately 1,135 liters per minute
  • 63.5 mm hose diameter Fire Retardant Drop System (FRDS Gen II)
  • Maximum drop of 3,500 liters in 20 seconds
  • Garmin G1000 NXi integrated glass cockpit

Approach

  • Led the conceptual design of the aircraft from mission requirements through to a defined configuration, including a matching plot to size wing loading and power loading against the design point.
  • Conducted longitudinal and lateral-directional stability analyses in XFLR5 — including static stability (Cm vs. α, lift curve, lift-to-drag ratio), neutral point determination, and CG travel diagram against forward/aft limits — to ensure the design met mission-compliant flight characteristics.
  • Evaluated dynamic stability modes (short period, phugoid, spiral, and rudder doublet response) at MTOW to confirm acceptable damping and handling qualities.
  • Derived the flight and gust envelope (V-n diagram) to define structural load limits across the stall-to-cruise speed range.
  • Ran flight simulations in X-Plane to evaluate control surface effectiveness and dynamic response under a range of loading scenarios, including a handling quality (bank angle) test rated on the Cooper-Harper scale.
  • Characterized stall behavior (altitude vs. stall speed) and climb/descent performance via ROC and ROD hodographs.

Results

Indrajal completed its flight tests, demonstrating dependable stability and control with the ability to operate from short or rough runways. Handling qualities were rated a 3 on the Cooper-Harper scale — mild, unpleasant deficiencies with only minimal pilot compensation required to achieve desired performance. Max climb angle reached 8.3° (ROCmax = 19 ft/s), with a minimum descent angle of -3.46°. With its 500 km range, 2-hour endurance, and 3,500-liter payload capacity, the aircraft is well-suited for quick, effective wildfire response.

Role

Led the conceptual design effort as part of a four-person team, working across sizing, stability analysis, and simulation-based validation of control response to translate mission constraints into a quantitative, flight-tested design.
